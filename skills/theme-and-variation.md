# Theme and Variation Form

## Purpose

Design a recognizable theme and a controlled set of variations.

## Use when

- design theme and variation form

## Do not use when

- when the requested result is a through-composed narrative without a recurring theme

## Inputs

- a section map, phrase labels, or a formal hypothesis
- harmonic and melodic landmarks

## Outputs

- formal labels and relationships
- candidate section or transition plans

## Procedure

1. Define the theme identity markers.
2. Choose a variation axis.
3. Plan how the final variation changes or confirms the theme.

## Hard constraints

- Every variation must remain traceable to the theme.
- Do not confuse a new section with a variation.

## Heuristics

- Vary one dimension at a time before combining axes.
- Use contrast to make recurrence meaningful.

## Validation status

P1 has no implemented engine validator for this card. Treat every result as advisory until P2 provides named operations and validators; do not describe an unimplemented check as performed.

## Failure modes

- Producing unrelated miniatures under one theme label.
- Letting every variation have identical pacing.

## Examples

- A theme varied by rhythm, harmony, and register.
- A final variation that transforms the theme.
