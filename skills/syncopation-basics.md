# Syncopation Basics

## Purpose

Place off-beat accents without destroying the perceived meter.

## Use when

- apply syncopation without losing pulse

## Do not use when

- when a strict unison pulse must remain mechanically unchanged

## Inputs

- meter, beat position, note values, or a rhythmic pattern
- a pulse or dance context when relevant

## Outputs

- a rhythmic interpretation
- candidate transformations that retain the source identity

## Procedure

1. Mark the stable pulse.
2. Choose a small number of off-beat accents.
3. Check each displacement and its resolution.

## Hard constraints

- Do not label every off-beat attack syncopation.
- Keep the underlying pulse audible in the explanation.

## Heuristics

- Use long-short patterns and repeated cells to create identity.
- Leave metric landmarks clear at phrase boundaries.

## Validation status

P1 has no implemented engine validator for this card. Treat every result as advisory until P2 provides named operations and validators; do not describe an unimplemented check as performed.

## Failure modes

- Accenting every subdivision until the pulse disappears.
- Calling a deliberate rest a syncopation without context.

## Examples

- A backbeat accent over a steady quarter-note pulse.
- A short off-beat figure that returns on the beat.
