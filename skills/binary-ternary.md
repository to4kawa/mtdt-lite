# Binary and Ternary Form

## Purpose

Map sections and compare returning material.

## Use when

- analyze binary or ternary form

## Do not use when

- when the form is through-composed and no recurring section can be justified

## Inputs

- a section map, phrase labels, or a formal hypothesis
- harmonic and melodic landmarks

## Outputs

- formal labels and relationships
- candidate section or transition plans

## Procedure

1. Identify the primary sections.
2. Compare reprise or contrasting material.
3. State the transition and ending evidence.

## Hard constraints

- Do not force a binary label onto a continuously changing form.
- Keep section length and function separate.

## Heuristics

- Use thematic return and cadence to support the label.
- Mark a partial or sectional return explicitly.

## Validation status

P1 has no implemented engine validator for this card. Treat every result as advisory until P2 provides named operations and validators; do not describe an unimplemented check as performed.

## Failure modes

- Counting bars without identifying function.
- Calling any repeated chorus section binary.

## Examples

- A repeated A section after a contrasting B section.
- A rounded binary with a partial return.
