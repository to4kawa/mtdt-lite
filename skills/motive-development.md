# Motive Development

## Purpose

Develop an existing motive while retaining recognizable identity.

## Use when

- develop a motive by sequence and imitation

## Do not use when

- when writing a new theme from scratch with no source motive

## Inputs

- a motive, phrase, or melodic contour
- a stylistic and tonal context

## Outputs

- contour observations
- candidate developments or phrase plans

## Procedure

1. State the motive interval and contour.
2. Choose a transformation type.
3. Check phrase placement and cadence.

## Hard constraints

- Do not replace the motive with an unrelated tune.
- Keep development proportional to the source character.

## Heuristics

- Use sequence to extend energy, not to repeat mechanically.
- Vary rhythm or interval selectively to create direction.

## Validation status

P1 has no implemented engine validator for this card. Treat every result as advisory until P2 provides named operations and validators; do not describe an unimplemented check as performed.

## Failure modes

- Losing the motive after one transformation.
- Overusing a sequence until the phrase loses contrast.

## Examples

- A two-note motive expanded by step sequence.
- A motive passed between voices with a new cadence.
