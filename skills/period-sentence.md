# Period and Sentence Analysis

## Purpose

Identify antecedent, consequent, and cadence function.

## Use when

- analyze a period or sentence phrase

## Do not use when

- when the phrase lacks a stable cadential or antecedent-consequent relation

## Inputs

- a section map, phrase labels, or a formal hypothesis
- harmonic and melodic landmarks

## Outputs

- formal labels and relationships
- candidate section or transition plans

## Procedure

1. Segment the phrase.
2. Compare presentation and continuation.
3. State the cadence evidence and any ambiguity.

## Hard constraints

- Do not label a period from length alone.
- Keep phrase function distinct from the larger section form.

## Heuristics

- Use harmonic arrival and thematic return as primary evidence.
- Report a mixed or weak reading when evidence conflicts.

## Validation status

P1 has no implemented engine validator for this card. Treat every result as advisory until P2 provides named operations and validators; do not describe an unimplemented check as performed.

## Failure modes

- Forcing a period onto a through-composed phrase.
- Ignoring a cadence that is syntactically implied.

## Examples

- An antecedent ending on a half cadence and consequent closure.
- A sentence with a stronger continuation.
